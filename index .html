How I work

Enterprise work has its own shape.

Users, business rules and product constraints — before any screen. What does the policy say? What does the payroll engine actually need?
Break the complexity into roles, workflows and states. Who initiates, who approves, who only needs to be told.
Information architecture and interaction hierarchy. Which action is primary on this screen, for this role, at this state.
Scalable interface systems: components first, screens as compositions, with AI-assisted exploration to try more directions early.
Usability testing on real flows, edge cases and error states — the month-end payroll, not the happy path.
Requirements grow after launch. The system is designed so the next module inherits the decisions of the last.

    I started by drawing screens. I stayed for the systems underneath them.

    I'm Dennis Joel Raj, a product designer in Chennai. For the last two years I've been the primary UI/UX designer at a startup building enterprise HRMS and ERP software — the kind of products where a wrong default in a leave policy screen turns into a payroll error a month later.

    Working as the sole designer meant owning everything: discovery with stakeholders, information architecture, the design system, high-fidelity UI for web and mobile, prototypes, usability testing and handoff. It also meant handling several products at once and learning to make decisions that hold across all of them.

    I now lead the design function. Most of my time goes to design direction, reviews and building the component library that lets the team move faster without drifting. I use Claude and Figma AI daily to explore more directions earlier — the judgement about which direction is right is still the job.
